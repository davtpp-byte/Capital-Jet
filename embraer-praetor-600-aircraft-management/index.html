Embraer Praetor 600 Overview

The Praetor 600 is Embraer’s super-midsize flagship, and it does something no other aircraft in its class can: fly nonstop across the Atlantic. Certified in 2019 and built on the same platform as the Legacy 500, it adds fuselage fuel tanks, aerodynamic winglets, and full-rated engines to reach a range of 4,018 nautical miles – enough to connect New York and London, or São Paulo and Miami, without a stop. It is the only super-midsize jet that can do so.

That range is matched by the technology that carries it. The Praetor 600 is one of the few aircraft in its class with full fly-by-wire flight controls, and the only one with active turbulence reduction – a system that smooths the ride in ways older designs cannot. It is powered by two Honeywell HTF7500E engines, part of the proven HTF7000 family that also powers the Challenger 300 and Gulfstream G280, and it flies behind the Rockwell Collins Pro Line Fusion flight deck, cruising at Mach 0.83 up to 45,000 feet.

Inside, the six-foot flat-floor cabin is among the most spacious in the super-midsize segment, seating up to nine passengers with a low cabin altitude that reduces fatigue on long flights. Embraer has since announced the Praetor 600E, an enhanced version with further cabin and safety upgrades, but the aircraft’s core strengths – intercontinental range, a smooth fly-by-wire ride, and a genuinely large cabin – are exactly the qualities that make knowledgeable management worthwhile.
