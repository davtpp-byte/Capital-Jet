Learjet 60 Overview

The Learjet became one of the most recognizable symbols of private aviation during the 1970s. Its cultural status was cemented when Carly Simon referenced a Learjet – flying to Nova Scotia to witness a total eclipse – in her 1972 hit “You’re So Vain.” Whether or not that song was ever about you, the Learjet has remained synonymous with executive travel, speed, and prestige ever since. The Learjet 60 continues that legacy, combining the brand’s heritage with proven performance, operational efficiency, and dependable economics.

The Learjet 60 is a midsize, medium-range business jet built by Bombardier Aerospace in Wichita, Kansas. It earned its FAA type certificate in January 1993 as a stretched, re-engined evolution of the Learjet 55, and it went on to become one of the most recognized midsize jets of its era. Production of the Model 60 ran through 2007; the upgraded 60XR followed, with manufacturing winding down by 2013. Bombardier announced the end of Learjet production in 2021 and delivered the final aircraft in March 2022. The Lear 60 is, in effect, the last of the legacy Learjets – and a large fleet of them is still flying.

What makes it a strong aircraft to own is the same thing that makes it worth managing well. Two Pratt & Whitney Canada PW305A turbofans, rated at roughly 4,600 pounds of thrust each, give the Lear 60 a genuine high-altitude climber’s profile: it reaches FL410 in around 18 minutes at max takeoff weight and cruises up near Mach 0.81.

When operated within its optimal mission profile, the Learjet 60 delivers approximately 2,400 nautical miles of range with four passengers and NBAA reserves, comfortably connecting major business centers such as New York and Miami, Toronto and Dallas, while enabling efficient same-day round trips across much of North America.

The cabin, offering about 5 feet 8 inches of stand-up height and seating for six to seven passengers, was the most spacious in the Learjet lineup at the time of its introduction, providing an exceptional balance of comfort, productivity, and performance.
