Advantages of Selling Your Aircraft Through an Operator

Aircraft Sales Led by an Operator, Not Just a Broker

The price an aircraft sells for is largely settled before an offer is signed. It depends on how complete the records are, what the pre-purchase inspection turns up, how many serious buyers see the aircraft, and what it costs to keep while the sale goes on. Sellers who work with an aircraft operator have an advantage at each of those points.

Capital Jets charters, manages, crews, and maintains aircraft under its own FAA Part 135 certificate. The people preparing your sale look at an aircraft the way a buyer’s team will, because evaluating aircraft is part of their daily work.

Operator-Led Sale vs. a Typical Brokered Listing

Here’s how an operator-led sale with Capital Jets compares with a typical brokered listing. The comparison reflects common practice in the market, not any specific company.

  • Records: with Capital Jets, the records are reviewed by people who maintain aircraft, and gaps are resolved or explained before buyers see them. In a typical brokered listing, they’re often shared just as the owner provides them, leaving issues for the buyer’s inspector to find.
  • Pre-purchase inspection: we anticipate likely findings and address them in advance. Without that preparation, findings can surface late and become grounds for a lower price.
  • Buyer reach: your aircraft is shown to end users, aircraft brokers, jet card providers and fractional operators through long-standing relationships, rather than relying mainly on public listing marketplaces.
  • While the aircraft is listed: qualifying aircraft can fly charter and earn revenue, instead of sitting idle while fixed costs continue.
  • Costs during the sale: you pay no management fees, no hangar fees, and no markup on outside maintenance. With a broker, you keep paying your current management company and hangar, and their fees and markups vary.
  • Accountability: one team handles everything from valuation to closing, so the work doesn’t pass between a broker, a manager, and a maintenance shop.

The Advantages in Detail

Your Aircraft Is Prepared by People Who Maintain Aircraft

A buyer’s inspector will go through the logbooks, the damage history, the inspection and airworthiness directive status, and the time left on major components. Our team brings decades of maintenance and flight-operations experience to that same review before the aircraft is listed. Missing entries, overdue items, and old repairs are found early, while you can still fix them or document them on your own terms.

Fewer Surprises at the Pre-Purchase Inspection

Most price renegotiation happens during the pre-purchase inspection, when unexpected findings give the buyer a reason to ask for a discount or extra work. Because we inspect and maintain aircraft as part of operating them, we know which findings tend to come up and can deal with them before the inspection starts. The price agreed at the offer stage is then far more likely to hold at closing.

Reach Beyond the Public Listing Sites

Many aircraft are sold through relationships rather than search results. Our long-standing contacts include end users, aircraft brokers, jet card providers, and legacy fractional operators, so your aircraft is shown to people already in the market for it. That means more qualified interest, and a sale that doesn’t have to be advertised widely if you prefer to keep it private.

Your Aircraft Can Earn While It’s for Sale

A sale can take months, and an idle aircraft keeps costing money. Qualifying aircraft can fly charter on our Part 135 certificate during that time. We run a floating fleet concentrated in the Northeast, one of the busiest business aviation markets in the US, so the aircraft goes wherever the next trip begins. Owners also earn from cancellation fees, standby charges, and non-flight-day fees, and qualifying aircraft can join a guaranteed utilization program that pays for agreed hours whether they’re flown or not. In a slow market, that income can make holding out for the right buyer a better choice than accepting a low offer.

Lower Costs While You Wait for the Right Buyer

If we manage the aircraft during the sale, you pay no management fees and no hangar fees. Outside maintenance is billed at the vendor’s own price, you choose the maintenance provider and parts supplier within FAA requirements, and fuel is priced competitively with nothing added for the owner.

Safety Credentials Buyers Recognize

Capital Jets is an ARGUS Platinum and WYVERN-verified operator. Those independent audits show buyers and their advisors that the team presenting the aircraft works to recognized safety and maintenance standards, which builds trust in what the records say.

A Realistic Price from the Start

An overpriced listing sits on the market, and the longer an aircraft goes unsold, the more buyers wonder why. We base our valuation on today’s market and tell you plainly what to expect, so the aircraft is priced to attract serious offers early instead of being reduced later.

One Team Accountable for the Whole Sale

Valuation, records, marketing, buyer screening, the pre-purchase inspection, escrow, and title transfer are handled by one team. You have one contact who knows the whole transaction, and nothing gets lost between separate companies.

Selling to Move Up to a New Aircraft?

When you’re replacing your aircraft, the hardest part is often the timing: selling too early can leave you without an aircraft, and buying too early means paying for two. Our aircraft acquisition team works alongside the sale, so the handover from one aircraft to the next is planned from the start.

Questions to Ask Before You Choose Who Sells Your Aircraft

The company that sells or manages your aircraft can cost you more than its commission if its incentives don’t match yours. Ask these questions before you sign with anyone, and compare the answers.

  • How does the company make its money? Some companies earn more from maintenance and markups than from operating aircraft, which can shape their advice. Capital Jets charges no markup on outside maintenance and no management or hangar fees.
  • Who reviews the records before the listing goes live? Problems found by the buyer’s inspector usually cost the seller money. We review the records before buyers see them.
  • Will my aircraft only appear on public listing sites? Private networks often reach more serious buyers. We present your aircraft to our network of end users, brokers, jet card providers, and fractional operators.
  • Can I choose my own maintenance provider and parts? Some companies require you to use their own shop. With Capital Jets, you choose, within FAA requirements.
  • What is the markup on outside maintenance and parts? Hidden markups reduce what you keep from the sale. We add none; you pay the vendor’s price.
  • Can the aircraft earn while it’s for sale? Months of idle costs can wipe out a higher sale price. Qualifying aircraft can fly charter with us, with guaranteed utilization in writing.
