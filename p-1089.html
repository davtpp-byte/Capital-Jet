Hawker 800XP, 850XP and 900XP Overview

Few midsize jets have been as widely flown as the Hawker 800 series. Descended from the British-built HS-125 that first flew in the 1960s, the line matured into one of the most successful and recognizable midsize families in business aviation – and three variants, the 800XP, 850XP, and 900XP, remain among the most active midsize aircraft in charter service today.

The Hawker 800XP – the “Extended Performance” model introduced in 1995 – is the volume leader of the family, with roughly 450 built and the most common variant of the Hawker family in North American charter service. Powered by two Honeywell TFE731-5BR engines, it offers a range of about 2,540 nautical miles, a maximum cruise speed near Mach 0.80, and a 41,000-foot ceiling. Its flat-floor, stand-up cabin seats eight passengers with a full galley and enclosed lavatory, and at six feet wide it is roomier than the midsize average – part of why the aircraft became such a fleet favorite.

The 850XP and 900XP built on that foundation. Certified in 2006, the 850XP is essentially the 800XP with blended winglets that add roughly 100 nautical miles of range, along with upgraded Collins Pro Line 21 avionics and a redesigned interior. The 900XP, introduced the following year, added uprated Honeywell TFE731-50R engines for stronger climb, better hot-and-high performance, and a longer range of roughly 2,800 nautical miles. All three share the same proven airframe and cabin, and all three benefit from a mature global support network – an advantage that, with the type no longer in production, makes knowledgeable management more valuable than ever.
